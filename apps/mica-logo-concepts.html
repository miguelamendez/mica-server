Mica mark study

Eight flat-color directions based on layered mineral sheets. The composition is rotated from the first draft: overlap now travels vertically. Squares remain diamond-shaped; hexagons explore a more crystalline silhouette.

All options avoid gradients, remain legible at 24 px, and can collapse to one color for terminal icons, favicons, and embossing.
01 · SQUARE

Mineral Layers

Quiet and architectural. Deep navy gives the server credibility; muted teal references natural mica without looking like generic AI branding.

Recommended balance
02 · SQUARE

Live Runtime

Brighter and more technical. Cobalt suggests computation while mint signals a healthy, live local service. Strongest choice for a dark interface.

Best UI visibility
03 · SQUARE

Core Monogram

Color-independent and serious. The dark overlap becomes the core—the orchestrator where model utilities meet. Easiest to reproduce anywhere.

Most timeless
04 · SQUARE

Warm Workshop

Human and tool-like rather than futuristic. Rust and ochre make Mica feel like a crafted local utility, with a subtle mineral and hardware character.

Most approachable
05 · HEXAGON

Crystal Stack

The hexagon feels naturally mineral and less like a generic app tile. Petrol and jade are restrained, distinctive, and compatible with both light and dark UI.

Recommended symbol
06 · HEXAGON

Terminal Signal

High-energy developer branding. Acid lime acts like a status LED against charcoal; the center dot represents the routing decision point.

Boldest technical
07 · HEXAGON

Instrument Grade

Ultramarine and muted gold feel precise and premium without using a gradient. Appropriate if Mica should read as serious infrastructure.

Most premium
08 · HEXAGON

Neutral Lattice

No brand color at all: just structure. The shared horizontal seam emphasizes layered materials and adapts automatically to any future product palette.

Most adaptable
